# KNCB 2026 Competition Rules and Dutch Cricket Context

Prepared 12 August 2026 from the English KNCB Competition Rules 2026 supplied by the user, the official Dutch 2026 rules, the KNCB regulations portal, KNCB annual reporting, the ICC, and the Dutch National Archives.

## 1. Authority and interpretation

1. The supplied English PDF is a 72-page translation of the Dutch `Competitiereglement 2026`, adopted by the KNCB Board on 13 April 2026.
2. The PDF itself says the Dutch version prevails in any inconsistency or conflict. The official Dutch document is therefore the controlling text.
3. The Competition Rules are not self-contained. A complete answer may also require the relevant 2026 Playing Conditions, the Player Points System Manual, Internal Regulations, Articles of Association, Disciplinary Regulations and sanctions guideline, Ground Regulations, MCC Laws, or an in-season KNCB decision/protocol.
4. A Board discretion clause is not an automatic entitlement. The Board decides dispensations, normally after advice from the Dispensation Committee.
5. Any conclusion about Article 15 (immigration/work status) must be verified against current Dutch government sources. The article expressly says no rights can be derived from its information.

## 2. Structure of the 2026 rules

- Definitions and general authority: definitions and Articles 1-7.
- Competition schedule and match administration: Articles 8-13.
- Player eligibility: Articles 14-21 and Article 18B.
- Dispensations and special player movements: Articles 22-28.
- Consequences of ineligibility: Article 29.
- Points, standings, championship, promotion and relegation: Articles 30-32.
- Umpires and scorers: Articles 33-38.
- Scoring, scorecards, team sheets and DLS: Articles 39-41B.
- Clothing, equipment and grounds: Articles 42-45.

The 2026 season runs from the second full weekend in April through the third full weekend in September. Men's One-Day and men's T20 are deliberately treated as separate competitions for player eligibility.

## 3. Competition map

- Men's One-Day: Topklasse, Hoofdklasse, Eerste Klasse, Tweede Klasse, Derde Klasse, Vierde Klasse.
- Men's T20: Topklasse, Hoofdklasse, Eerste Klasse, T20 Lower Divisions.
- Men's recreational One-Day: ZAMI and ZOMI.
- Women's One-Day: Topklasse and Hoofdklasse.
- Women's T20: one division in the supplied rules.
- Youth: U9, U11, U13, U15 and U17, governed by separate youth rules and playing conditions.
- `THE Divisions` means Topklasse, Hoofdklasse and Eerste Klasse.

## 4. Eligibility and dispensation framework

### Article 14 - general eligibility

- The player must be registered as a KNCB member of a club; the membership name must exactly match ID.
- A player normally plays for only one club per competition. A player may represent Club A in One-Day and Club B in T20 if properly a member of both and, where needed, transferred.
- Limited late-registration exceptions exist outside men's THE divisions; separate occasional-player treatment applies in women's and men's recreational competitions.
- Normally only one team may be represented on a Match Day. A youth exception applies to a player born on or after 1 September 2007.

### Article 15 - non-EU/EEA players and coaching

- The rule distinguishes Topklasse from lower levels and playing from work/coaching.
- KNCB does not itself validate immigration or employment compliance.
- The legal consequences can be serious and the rule directs clubs to current IND, UWV and tax authority information.

### Article 16 - transfer between clubs

- Prior KNCB permission is required if the player played for another club in the current or either of the two prior seasons.
- The ordinary transfer deadline is 15 February and prior-season financial obligations to the former club must be met.
- Late dispensation is possible only on stated special grounds. Playing without an approved transfer makes the player ineligible.

### Article 17 - men's THE squad lists

- Separate One-Day and T20 squad lists: 1 April and 11 June deadlines respectively.
- Maximum 30 players for one THE team, 50 for two, 60 for three.
- Lists normally cannot be amended after the deadline. Narrow youth and special-circumstance routes exist.

### Article 18 - KNCB selection replacement and Hoofdklasse promotion decision match

- A properly notified lower-team player replacing a player selected for a KNCB representative side can return without the usual dispensation, subject to timing, registration, squad-list and Saturday/Sunday restrictions.
- For the Hoofdklasse promotion decision match, the ordinary threshold is at least 50% of the team's double round-robin matches (7 in 2026), with specific adjustments and exceptions for KNCB call-ups, established lower-level players, qualifying youth, injury and special circumstances.
- A player not on the squad list cannot use dispensation to enter the decision match.

### Article 18B - Player Points System

- New in 2026 for men's THE teams.
- Each squad-list player receives points; the playing XI has a combined cap of 28.
- The Player Points System Manual is expressly incorporated into the Competition Rules and must be checked separately.

### Articles 19-21 - women, lower divisions and recreation

- Women's competitions have only Article 14's additional eligibility rules in this document because of the 2026 team structure, but other regulations still apply.
- A squad-listed THE player generally needs dispensation to play in the same competition's Lower Divisions, unless the established-lower-player, long-membership, qualifying-youth or selection-replacement exceptions apply.
- Recreational One-Day is deliberately permissive. Article 22 and the Saturday/Sunday ban do not apply, although KNCB gives Spirit of Cricket recommendations for dominant THE-level substitutes (bat at 7 or lower/retire at 50; at most four overs or stop after the third wicket, whichever comes first).

### Article 22 - downward movement in men's One-Day

- One-Day appearances across teams are aggregated.
- Once the player reaches 50% or more of the pre-season scheduled matches, the player cannot play in a lower division than the lowest already played without prior dispensation.
- Only matches where the toss occurred count; substitute-only fielding does not.
- If the club has multiple teams in the same division, stricter same-division thresholds replace the 50% rule: 4 matches in one team where there are two club teams, or 3 where there are three or more.

### Article 23 - downward/other-team movement in men's T20

- In THE, the ordinary downward THE threshold is 7 matches in one team.
- After 2 THE T20 matches in one or more teams, prior dispensation is required to play T20 in the Lower Divisions.
- In the Lower Divisions' First Stage, after 2 matches in one team, prior dispensation is required to play in any other Lower-Division T20 team, irrespective of relative team ranking.
- Selection-replacement exceptions and Article 24 still need separate checking.

### Article 24 - the seven-day (Saturday/Sunday) ban

- Despite its name, it covers the seven calendar days after the Match Day and can apply to weekday/holiday matches.
- It is competition-specific: One-Day and T20 are separate.
- In THE, a player cannot move to a lower-division team in the same competition during the seven-day window without prior dispensation, subject to youth and established-lower-team exceptions.
- In Lower-Division One-Day, special rules apply to predominantly Saturday teams and multiple club teams in the same division.
- In Lower-Division T20, a player cannot play for two different T20 teams within the seven-day window without prior dispensation.
- Recreational One-Day is excluded from this ban.

### Articles 25-27 - special situations

- Playing for a foreign club in a foreign league/tournament during the Dutch season normally requires prior dispensation before returning to KNCB competition. National-team ECN participation is treated differently from foreign-club participation; timing before/after the Dutch season also matters.
- A club can seek one Designated Player for its first men's THE team, subject to coaching qualification, work permission where required, full-season role and deadlines. Replacement is narrowly regulated.
- Women may play in men's competitions; men may not play in women's competitions.

### Article 28 - procedure

Every application must be:

- for one competition only;
- for one team only;
- for one player only;
- submitted by a club board member on the prescribed form by email to the Competition Manager;
- complete, with all supporting documents; and
- normally submitted at least three working days before it is intended to take effect.

Only the Board grants or rejects dispensation. The decision identifies competition, team, effective date and conditions. A downward-movement dispensation lapses immediately if the player again plays for the former/higher team(s), or another team in that competition, except a Recreational Division team. A second application for the same destination team is said to be almost always advised against.

### Article 29 - ineligible player consequences

- Ordinary match: the result is adjusted as prescribed and points are deducted so the net competitive sanction is two match points.
- Final: the team fielding the ineligible player loses; the ordinary result/points formula does not apply.
- Club fine: EUR 100 per ineligible player, per affected match.
- Measures apply to every affected match and are published.
- Appeal goes to the Appeals Committee, which reviews only whether the Board's decision was reasonable.

## 5. Practical method for answering a player case

For every question, establish all of the following before reaching a conclusion:

1. Competition: One-Day or T20.
2. Gender category and division: THE, Lower, Recreational or women's.
3. Club/team identity and relative team ranking.
4. Current and prior-season club membership, transfer history and exact registered identity.
5. Squad-list status for that specific competition.
6. Every relevant appearance: date, team, division, whether toss occurred, and whether substitute-only.
7. Pre-season scheduled match count for the relevant team/pool.
8. Any same-division multiple-team situation.
9. Any appearance in the preceding seven calendar days.
10. KNCB selection/call-up and replacement notifications.
11. Foreign-club competition/tournament participation during the season.
12. Age/date of birth and historical lower-team participation.
13. Existing dispensation: exact competition, team, effective date, conditions and whether it has lapsed.
14. Player Points classification and the proposed XI total where Article 18B applies.

Eligibility is the intersection of all applicable rules. Passing one threshold does not override another article.

## 6. Known drafting and translation cautions

- The English translation contains several obvious cross-reference/wording errors, including duplicated Article 24 references where Articles 22-24 are intended, a 2024 reference in the 2026 women's T20 rain section, and other minor typographical issues.
- The English 72-page and Dutch 74-page layouts do not have matching page numbers.
- The Dutch text must be checked for any disputed phrase.
- In-season Board announcements (for example a weather/heat protocol) can affect match administration and should be checked for the date in question.

## 7. Dutch cricket and KNCB historical timeline

- Early 19th century: cricket was introduced in the Netherlands by British soldiers during the Napoleonic era.
- 1860s: cricket had become a significant Dutch sport.
- 1881: the Netherlands played an early representative match, using 22 players against Uxbridge CC.
- 30 September 1883: the Nederlandsche Cricket Bond was founded.
- 1884: the first national competition was held; Haagsche CC won.
- From 1886: English touring sides increasingly visited.
- 1934: the Nederlandse Dames Cricket Bond is described by KNCB historical material as founded (the National Archives fonds labels its archive 1932-1985, so the distinction between precursor records and formal founding should be preserved).
- 28 June 1958: the association received the `Koninklijk` designation; it was renewed in 2019 through 7 May 2037.
- 1964: the Netherlands recorded its celebrated victory over Australia.
- 1966: the Netherlands became an ICC Associate Member.
- 1979-2005: the men's side appeared in every ICC Trophy in that span, finishing runner-up in 1986 and 1990 and winning in 2001.
- 1985: the KNCB and the Dutch women's association merged, bringing senior, junior, men's and women's cricket into one governing body.
- Women's World Cups: participation in 1988, 1993, 1997 and 2000.
- Men's ODI World Cups: participation in 1996, 2003, 2007, 2011 and 2023.
- 2009: famous last-ball T20 World Cup victory over England at Lord's.
- 2011: KNCB announced its first professional player contracts/agreements.
- 2014: the Netherlands reached the T20 World Cup Super 10 after the rapid chase against Ireland and defeated England again.
- 2022: the men qualified for the T20 World Cup, finished in the top eight and thereby qualified automatically for 2024; women's ODI status was expanded to include the Netherlands.
- 2023: the men returned to the ODI World Cup after a notable qualifying campaign.
- 2025-2026: player participation grew, the men qualified for the 2026 T20 World Cup, the women reached their first Women's T20 World Cup in 2026, and KNCB launched an academy intended to bridge club and international cricket.

## 8. Institutional understanding

KNCB is both a domestic competition regulator and the national governing body for Dutch cricket. It organizes league structures, eligibility, officiating, statistics, club support, youth development and national/high-performance pathways. Its model has a persistent tension that explains many 2026 rules: Dutch cricket benefits from international and overseas expertise, but a small club ecosystem is vulnerable to imported-player concentration, strategic movement between club teams, and international call-up disruption. The rules therefore combine openness with level-playing-field controls: squad lists, Player Points, match-count thresholds, seven-day restrictions, foreign-competition permissions, selection-replacement exceptions and narrowly scoped dispensations.

The strongest documented archival corpus is the Dutch National Archives KNCB collection (1883-2009), containing governance and competition records, player match sheets, publications, photographs, and the full women's association archive. The KNCB Archive and Library Committee continues this work and the Kleefstra Almanak remains the annual statistical reference.
